👩‍🏫 Teacher only · sample answer (about 1½ min)

Britain enters written history in 55 BC, when Julius Caesar crossed the Channel, but the real Roman invasion began in AD 43. Within forty years the Romans had conquered most of England and Wales. Not everyone accepted them: in AD 60 Boudica led a revolt and burnt London. In AD 122 Hadrian ordered a wall across the north, and its remains still stand today. As the empire went into decline, the Romans left, and by 410 Britain had to defend itself. Angles, Saxons and Jutes sailed in from Germany and Denmark and set up their own kingdoms; the word England has its origin in the Angles. Then came the Vikings. They attacked Lindisfarne in 793, and by the 870s they had taken over much of the north and east. They almost wiped out the English kingdoms, but Alfred the Great held on in Wessex, and his grandson Athelstan became the first King of all England in 927. Finally, in 1066 Edward the Confessor died without children, Harold became king, and William of Normandy defeated him at Hastings.

Listen for: the four stages in order · all 8 words · by + date + had + V3 (by the 870s they had taken over) · past simple for dated events.

📏 The rule: every linker needs something before it
TypeLinkersNeeds before it
Contrasthowever, but, yet, even so, despite this, in spite of this, althoughan opposite idea: failure → this time, however, success
Earliera year earlier, before that, by then, by the time, until thena date or event; the verb is usually had + V3
Same timewhile, meanwhile, at the same timeanother action going on
Laterthen, later, after that, two years later, eventually, in the end, finally, since thenan earlier stage; eventually / finally = the end of a long struggle; since then + has / have + V3
Resultas a result, so, therefore, in this way, that is whya cause
Additionalso, moreover, in addition, what is morea similar point
🇷🇺 Eventually — это не «эвентуально», а «в конце концов». Despite без of: despite the storm = in spite of the storm. После despite — существительное или -ing, а после although — целое предложение: although there was a storm.

Who is he? · 12 min

Прочитайте текст и выполните задание к нему.

📖 the answer may not be in the text✍️ spelling countsfollow the format

He was born in London in about 1343, the son of a successful wine merchant. As a teenager he became a page in the household of the Countess of Ulster, the wife of one of the king’s sons, and there he learned the manners and the French of the court.

In 1359 he went to fight in France, in the long war between the two countries that had begun in 1337. He was captured near Reims, and the following year King Edward III himself paid sixteen pounds towards his ransom. For the rest of his life he served the royal family as a soldier, a messenger and a diplomat, travelling to France, Spain and Italy, where he discovered the works of Dante and Petrarch.

In 1374 he was given a well-paid job as a customs official in the Port of London, checking the wool that was sent to Europe. He did this work for twelve years, and in his free time he wrote poetry. At that time most educated people in England wrote in French or Latin. He chose the English spoken in London, and his poems helped to turn it into a language for serious literature.

His most famous work, which he began in the late 1380s, is a collection of stories told by a group of pilgrims on their way to the tomb of an archbishop who had been murdered in his own cathedral in 1170. The pilgrims, twenty-nine of them, meet at an inn called the Tabard in Southwark, just south of London Bridge. The innkeeper suggests a competition: each traveller will tell stories on the way, and the best storyteller will win a free dinner. The pilgrims come from every part of society: a knight, a miller, a cook, a nun, a merchant and a woman from Bath who has had five husbands. He never finished the work: only twenty-four of the planned tales were written.

He died in 1400 and was buried in Westminster Abbey. Over the centuries so many writers were buried or remembered near him that this part of the abbey got a name of its own. Nearly eighty years after his death, his stories were among the first books ever printed in England.

💡Answer · open after you’ve guessed

On the Ugra, the Golden Horde turned back without a battle, and Ivan III stopped paying tribute: the end of the Mongol-Tatar yoke. At Bosworth, Richard III was killed and Henry Tudor became king: the end of the Wars of the Roses. In both countries a strong new monarchy began within five years, and historians often use these dates for the end of the Middle Ages.

Medieval England, 1066–1485

After his victory at Hastings, William the Conqueror had to hold down a hostile country. He built castles everywhere, among them the White Tower in London, and gave English land to his Norman lords. In 1086 he ordered a great survey of his kingdom, the Domesday Book, which recorded who owned every farm, every mill and even every pig.

In 1154 Henry II became the first Plantagenet king. He ruled an empire that stretched from the Scottish border to the Pyrenees and included half of France. During his reign he quarrelled with his old friend Thomas Becket, the Archbishop of Canterbury, and in 1170 four of the king’s knights murdered Becket in his own cathedral.

Henry’s son John lost most of the French lands and angered the barons with heavy taxes. In 1215 they forced him to agree to Magna Carta, which said that even the king had to obey the law. John’s grandson Edward I conquered Wales in 1283 and built huge castles there to control it.

In 1337 Edward III claimed the French crown, and the Hundred Years’ War broke out. Eleven years later a far worse enemy arrived: the Black Death, which wiped out between a third and half of the population. In 1415 Henry V won a famous victory at Agincourt, but by 1453 the English had lost almost all of their lands in France.

Two years later a civil war began between two branches of the royal family, the houses of Lancaster and York. Today it is known as the Wars of the Roses. It ended in 1485 at Bosworth, where Richard III was killed and Henry Tudor took the crown. England’s medieval age was over, and the age of the Tudors had begun.
